# Development summary: 2026-11-04

## Overview

NystagmuSense is testing whether a consumer iPhone can **reliably measure and
detect optokinetic nystagmus (OKN)**, using ARKit face and eye tracking
(TrueDepth, about 60 Hz) while it shows moving stripes. The long-term aim is
standardised contrast-sensitivity experiments.

**Where things stand.** Recording is reliable, and recordings now reach the Mac
automatically with descriptive names and fuller metadata. OKN-like sawtooth eye
movements are visible in the data. The offline detector (`detect_okn.py`) is now
the main bottleneck, and the next step is to label resets by hand so it can be
tuned and evaluated against human-reviewed labels. Nothing is clinically
validated.

## Experimental findings

**Pilot 01** (2026-11-04, one participant, 21 recordings; see
`analysis/pilot_01/report.md` in #10):
- **A vs B/C:** every free-viewing OKN trial (5 of 5) showed an OKN-like
  sawtooth, and none of the 11 fixation-control trials did.
- **Detector:** it found only 1–2 beats per OKN trial and no OKN-positive
  windows. Its 40 deg/s threshold misses most visible resets.
- **Sign:** deliberate left/right gaze trials showed the detector's documented
  sign convention is probably inverted.

**Direction reversal (today, one trial per direction, separate from Pilot 01):**

| Recording | Stripes | Slow phases / resets (detector angle convention) | Visible reset-like events |
|---|---|---|---|
| `DIR_T005_OKN_RIGHT_20261104_074541` | rightward, 126 pt/s | negative-going drift, upward resets | about 4 |
| `DIR_T006_OKN_LEFT_20261104_074607` | leftward, 126 pt/s | positive-going drift, downward resets | about 11 |

The two traces are roughly mirror images. That is preliminary evidence of
stimulus-driven OKN, and it fits the inverted sign found in Pilot 01. With only
one trial per direction, it isn't statistically meaningful. `detect_okn.py`
still finds just 1 fast phase and 0 accepted beats in each trial.

## Current limitations

- The fast-phase detector misses many resets, and its 40 deg/s velocity
  threshold hasn't been tuned.
- Human-reviewed reset labels aren't done yet. The tooling exists, but the
  labelling hasn't started.
- The detector's sign convention needs correcting and verifying. It is
  unchanged in code.
- Only one leftward and one rightward trial exist, all from one participant, so
  there is no cross-participant validation.
- There's no independent eye-tracking reference (a lab eye tracker or EOG), so
  the labels describe events visible in ARKit data.
- The stimulus background fades during each trial, which could confound the
  results.
- The distance estimate is approximate, from camera to eyes, and isn't
  formally calibrated.

## Next steps (planned, not started)

1. **Human annotation.** Label fast-phase resets in Pilot 01 with
   `scripts/annotate_okn.py`. Do the held-out evaluation trials first, with
   suggestions hidden, then the development trials in assisted mode. The plan
   is in `analysis/annotations/README.md` (#12).
2. **Detector optimisation**, using the reviewed labels:
   - try fast-phase velocity thresholds from about 10 to 40 deg/s;
   - report event-level precision, recall and F1 with `evaluate_okn.py`;
   - improve reset detection and slow-phase segmentation;
   - correct and verify the sign convention;
   - evaluate the final settings once, on held-out recordings.
3. **Pilot 02 preparation**, once the detector improves:
   - both stimulus directions;
   - standardised viewing distance;
   - constant stimulus brightness and contrast;
   - a balanced trial order;
   - repeat sessions, then more participants.
